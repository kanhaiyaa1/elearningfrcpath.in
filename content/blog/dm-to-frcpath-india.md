An MD Pathology graduate in India usually faces two serious options: a super-specialty degree through NEET-SS or INI-SS, and the Fellowship of the Royal College of Pathologists. This DM Pathology to FRCPath roadmap explains how the two can be sequenced instead of treated as alternatives. The two qualifications serve different purposes, and many candidates now plan both, so the useful question is how to order the stages and spend the available months.

We separate what is verified from what is our own planning advice. Dates, fees and centre lists come from the Royal College of Pathologists (RCPath) publications checked in October 2026. The sequencing suggestions are our experience. Where eligibility or rules matter, confirm them with the College and with the relevant Indian counselling authority, because both change.

---

## What Each Qualification Gives You

| Qualification | What it is | What it supports |
|---|---|---|
| DM or DrNB in a pathology super-specialty (via NEET-SS or INI-SS) | An Indian super-specialty degree | Faculty roles, super-specialty reporting posts in India |
| FRCPath | A UK Royal College fellowship, examined in two parts | UK specialist training and consultant routes, and international recognition |
| Both | Indian specialty depth plus a UK credential | Flexibility between India and abroad, and clinical or academic roles |

FRCPath does not replace the Indian degree for Indian faculty posts, and a DM does not carry UK recognition by itself. Neither guarantees a job. UK work additionally needs GMC registration and a right to work, which are separate from the exam.

On the UK side, the BMA's published 2026 scale for consultants in England on the 2003 contract runs from £113,565 at the starting threshold to £150,569 after 14 years. Use that only as context for the destination, not as a forecast of what any candidate will earn.

## The Timeline in Stages

```
MD Pathology
    ↓
Stage 1: Build the common base (MD years)
    ↓
Stage 2: NEET-SS / INI-SS entrance
    ↓
Stage 3: FRCPath Part 1 (once eligible)
    ↓
Stage 4: FRCPath Part 2 (during or after DM)
    ↓
Stage 5: Choose a career route
```

The exam windows for FRCPath do not move to fit your degree, so plan backward from the published dates.

## Stage 1: Build the Common Base During MD

The core of what both routes test is the same: systemic pathology, the WHO classification, molecular pathology and immunohistochemistry. During MD, treat your reporting and grossing work as exam preparation. Learn to describe slides in the structured way that practical examinations reward, and keep up with WHO 5th edition changes. There is no official figure for how much content overlaps between NEET-SS and FRCPath Part 1, so we do not quote one. The overlap is substantial in oncopathology and molecular pathology, and smaller in areas such as UK autopsy practice and UK reporting conventions.

## Stage 2: NEET-SS and INI-SS

Decide which entrance exams fit your list of target institutions. NEET-SS is run by NBEMS and INI-SS by AIIMS on behalf of the participating institutes. Their patterns, seat numbers and marking details are set in each year's notice, so we do not compare them number by number here. Our post on [NEET-SS versus INI-SS](/blog/neet-ss-vs-neet-ini-ss-2026-pathology-entrance-comparison/) and the [INI-SS pathology guide](/blog/ini-ss-pathology-2026-guide/) set out the choice, and the [NEET SS pathology study guide](/blog/neet-ss-pathology-study-guide/) gives a six-month content plan.

If you also plan FRCPath, use one resource list for common content and keep timed practice separate, because the formats differ. The sister site compares the two exams in its [NEET-SS versus FRCPath](https://neetsspathology.com/vs-frcpath) page.

## Stage 3: FRCPath Part 1

### Format and eligibility

RCPath describes histopathology Part 1 as 125 multiple-choice questions, a mixture of one-best-answer and extended-matching formats, in 3 hours. From Autumn 2026 it is taken on a computer in British Council test centres, with content and pass standard unchanged. Eligibility, as reported in 2024, required a medical degree, at least one year of specialty training and supervisor confirmation, so most Indian candidates become eligible after the first year of MD. Confirm the current regulations before you apply.

### Windows and dates

RCPath runs two sittings a year. For Autumn 2026, applications ran from 29 May to 3 July and Part 1 was taken between 21 and 25 September, with results on 20 November. For Spring 2026, applications ran from 28 November 2025 to 2 January 2026, with the exam in March. Dates for Spring 2027 had not been published when we checked, so watch the College's calendar around the turn of the year.

### Where to sit

Autumn 2026 Part 1 centres included New Delhi, as well as Karachi, Amman, Singapore, Colombo and Yangon, along with five centres in the UK and Ireland. The College allocates the nearest centre to your registered address and can cancel a centre with fewer than 10 candidates, so keep travel flexible.

### Fee

The 2026 Part 1 fee is £749. Our post on [FRCPath exam fees](/blog/frcpath-exam-fees/) covers the whole budget, and [how hard Part 1 is](/blog/how-hard-is-frcpath-part-1/) covers difficulty and preparation.

### When in your training

Many candidates attempt Part 1 once eligible and before a heavy DM workload begins, because the knowledge is fresh and clinical duties are lighter. This is a planning view, not a rule. A candidate who joins DM immediately may prefer to prepare in the first year, with a fixed daily block of two hours and timed screen practice in the final month.

## Stage 4: FRCPath Part 2

Part 2 histopathology is a practical examination with six components over two days: surgical histology, cytopathology, long cases, macroscopic pathology, OSPE stations and frozen sections. Daily DM work in oncopathology or haematopathology is useful rehearsal for several of these components, but the long cases and macroscopic stations still need deliberate practice in the College's format.

The 2026 Part 2 fee is £1,576. For Autumn 2026 the College published Part 2 histopathology dates of 13 and 14 October. Part 2 is held in the UK and in a small number of overseas centres, which the College's international pages list as Irbid, Cairo and Dubai, and the College has invited more centres to apply to host Part 2 from Autumn 2026. We found no announcement of an India centre. Our earlier post on [overseas Part 2 centres](/blog/frcpath-part2-histopath-overseas-autumn-2026/) discusses what the expansion could mean for Indian candidates, and you should check the College's current centre list before you commit to travel plans.

Eligibility and the training required before Part 2 are set in the College regulations. Confirm them in the current document for your sitting and plan to be ready after enough supervised reporting experience, not just after passing Part 1.

## Stage 5: Career Routes After Both

| Route | What helps | What else is needed |
|---|---|---|
| Indian faculty or senior reporting posts | The DM or DrNB, plus FRCPath as an added credential | Institution requirements and recruitment rules |
| UK specialist training and consultant routes | FRCPath, training experience | GMC registration, right to work, a competitive post |
| Other international posts | FRCPath and super-specialty experience | Local licensing rules in the country concerned |
| Academic and research work | A broader network and credential combination | Publications and posts as usual |

Read our post on [careers after DM Oncopathology](/blog/dm-oncopathology-career-neet-ss-pathology-2026/) for the Indian side. We deliberately avoid salary promises for other countries, because they vary by employer, licensing route and year.

## A Combined Budget

Only part of the cost is fixed. The RCPath fees are £749 for Part 1 and £1,576 for Part 2, a total of £2,325 for a first-attempt pass in both. Entrance exam application fees are set in the NBEMS and INI-SS notices each year, and travel and study costs depend on you. Budget these groups separately and re-check fees every year, because they change.

---

## Putting It Together: A Sample Sequence

This is one possible sequence, not a rule.

1. **MD, year 1 to 2:** common base and early Part 1 preparation once eligible.
2. **MD, final year:** NEET-SS or INI-SS entrance preparation and the Part 1 application if you choose to sit.
3. **DM, year 1:** Part 1 if not already done, with a fixed daily study block.
4. **DM, year 2 to 3:** Part 2 preparation in the College format, aligned with the sitting dates.
5. **After DM:** choose your route.

For another take on running both exams in one cycle, see our [NEET-SS and FRCPath dual plan](/blog/frcpath-autumn-2026-application-neet-ss-dual-plan/). For structured preparation material, the [FRCPath Part 1 histopathology course](https://elearningfrcpath.com/frcpath-part-1-histopathology-course), the [NEET-SS pathology course](https://elearningfrcpath.com/neet-ss-pathology) and the [pathology MCQ bank](https://elearningfrcpath.com/pathology-mcq-2026) cover both exams.

## Practice MCQs

**Q1.** A bone marrow biopsy shows small lymphoid cells with abundant pale cytoplasm and fine projections. The cells are positive for CD103, CD25 and annexin A1, and the bone marrow aspirate is a dry tap. Which mutation is characteristic?

- A) JAK2 V617F
- B) BRAF V600E
- C) NPM1
- D) MYD88 L265P

<details>
<summary>View Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: B — BRAF V600E**

The picture is hairy cell leukaemia, which carries BRAF V600E in nearly all classic cases. Annexin A1 is a useful marker that separates it from other small B-cell neoplasms. JAK2 V617F is associated with myeloproliferative neoplasms, NPM1 with AML, and MYD88 L265P with lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma.

</details>

**Q2.** A pleural biopsy shows a malignant epithelioid proliferation. Which immunohistochemistry panel result supports mesothelioma instead of metastatic adenocarcinoma?

- A) Calretinin and WT1 positive, MOC31 and Ber-EP4 negative
- B) TTF-1 and napsin A positive
- C) MOC31 and Ber-EP4 positive, calretinin negative
- D) CDX2 positive

<details>
<summary>View Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: A — Calretinin and WT1 positive, MOC31 and Ber-EP4 negative**

Mesothelial markers (calretinin, WT1, D2-40, CK5/6) with negative epithelial markers (MOC31, Ber-EP4) support mesothelioma. TTF-1 and napsin A suggest lung adenocarcinoma, MOC31 and Ber-EP4 positivity suggests adenocarcinoma, and CDX2 suggests gastrointestinal origin.

</details>

**Q3.** A non-smoking woman has a lung adenocarcinoma with an EGFR exon 19 deletion. Which treatment class is indicated?

- A) EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor
- B) ALK inhibitor
- C) Anti-PD-1 monotherapy alone
- D) BRAF inhibitor

<details>
<summary>View Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: A — EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitor**

EGFR exon 19 deletions and the L858R mutation predict response to EGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors. ALK inhibitors are for ALK-rearranged tumours, BRAF inhibitors for BRAF V600E tumours, and EGFR-mutant adenocarcinomas typically respond less well to anti-PD-1 monotherapy.

</details>

## FAQs

**Can I do FRCPath while doing a DM in India?**

Many candidates plan to, but you must meet RCPath's eligibility and training requirements, and your DM workload limits study time. Part 1 is usually attempted early, once eligible, and Part 2 later in training or after DM. Check the College regulations and confirm with your department how much leave you can take for exam days.

**When can I sit FRCPath Part 1 from India?**

RCPath runs Part 1 twice a year. For Autumn 2026, applications ran from 29 May to 3 July and the exam took place between 21 and 25 September at British Council test centres, including New Delhi. Spring sittings run in March, with applications around the turn of the year. Spring 2027 dates had not been published when we checked.

**Is there a Part 2 centre in India?**

We found no announcement of an India centre for Part 2 histopathology. The College delivers Part 2 in the UK and in a small number of overseas centres, listed on its international pages as Irbid, Cairo and Dubai, and has invited more centres to apply from Autumn 2026. Check the current list before planning travel.

**How much does the dual qualification cost in exam fees?**

RCPath's 2026 histopathology fees are £749 for Part 1 and £1,576 for Part 2, so £2,325 for a first-attempt pass in both. NEET-SS and INI-SS fees are set in each year's notice, and travel and study costs vary. Re-check each figure before you pay, because fees change every year.

**Does FRCPath guarantee a UK job?**

No. FRCPath is a qualification and one part of a UK career route. UK posts also require GMC registration, a right to work and success in competitive recruitment. Consultant pay in England is set by a published scale, which the BMA lists from £113,565 to £150,569 for 2003-contract consultants in 2026, but your own path will depend on your post and experience.

## Key Takeaways

- A DM and FRCPath are different credentials that can be sequenced, not forced into a single choice.
- Plan backward from the RCPath application windows. Autumn 2026 Part 1 ran 21 to 25 September, with applications from 29 May to 3 July.
- Part 1 is a 125-question, 3-hour computer-based exam, with New Delhi among the Autumn 2026 centres.
- Part 2 is a six-component practical, held in the UK and a few overseas centres. No India centre had been announced when we checked.
- The 2026 RCPath fees total £2,325 for a first-attempt pass in both parts.
- Neither qualification guarantees a job, and UK roles need GMC registration and a right to work.
