If you are a pathology trainee in India or elsewhere outside the UK, the first practical question about the Fellowship is simple: what will FRCPath exam fees add up to? For 2026 the Royal College of Pathologists lists £749 for Part 1 and £1,576 for Part 2 in histopathology, so the examination fees alone come to £2,325 for a candidate who passes both parts at the first attempt. The rest of the budget depends on where you sit each exam, how you study and how many attempts you need.

This post sets out the verified fees, the costs that sit around them, and a planning method that works for overseas candidates. Every fee quoted here was checked against the College's published 2026 material in October 2026. Fees are revised every year, so treat the figures as a planning base and confirm them on the RCPath website before you pay.

---

## FRCPath Exam Fees for 2026 (Histopathology)

| Item | 2026 fee | Note |
|---|---|---|
| FRCPath Part 1 | £749 | Written exam, 125 questions, 3 hours |
| FRCPath Part 2 | £1,576 | Practical exam, six components over two days |
| **Both parts, first attempt** | **£2,325** | Exam fees only |

Two points about these numbers deserve attention before you plan around them.

First, the fee rises each year. A published RCPath notice from April 2024 gave the Part 1 fee as £707. The 2026 fee is £749, an increase of £42 in two years. If you plan to sit in 2027 or later, add a margin rather than assuming the 2026 figure will hold.

Second, the fee is charged per sitting. Whether you pay again for a re-sit, and how component re-sits for Part 2 are priced, is set out in the College's examination regulations and fee schedule. We have not reproduced re-sit figures here because we could not confirm them from the current documents. Read the 2026 regulations before you apply, particularly if you are re-entering after a failed attempt.

## Costs Around the Fee

The exam fee is the most visible cost, but it is rarely the largest one for an international candidate. Budget in five groups.

| Cost group | What it covers | How to estimate |
|---|---|---|
| Exam fees | Part 1 and Part 2 | £749 + £1,576 (verified) |
| Travel for Part 1 | Journey to a British Council test centre | Depends on your distance from the centre |
| Travel for Part 2 | Flights and stay near the centre | Get current quotes for your dates |
| Study resources | Textbooks, WHO Blue Books, question banks, courses | Your own shortlist plus subscription prices |
| Time away from work | Leave taken for the exam days | Your own salary and leave policy |

For illustration only, at an exchange rate of ₹110 to the pound, £2,325 is about ₹2.56 lakh. Use the rate on the day you pay, because card charges and exchange movement can change the final amount by a noticeable margin.

### Part 1: where you sit and what it costs you to get there

From the Autumn 2026 sitting, RCPath delivers Part 1 on a computer in secure British Council test centres, instead of the earlier remote format. The exam content, number of questions and pass standard are unchanged. The Autumn 2026 centres published by the College are London, Manchester, Edinburgh, Bristol and Dublin in the UK and Ireland, and New Delhi, Karachi, Amman, Singapore, Colombo and Yangon internationally. Amman rotates with Kuwait and Qatar, and Singapore rotates with Hong Kong between autumn and spring.

For a candidate in North India, New Delhi means the Part 1 travel cost is a domestic train or flight and a hotel night or two. Candidates in the south or east of India will spend more on domestic travel. Centres run only if enough candidates enter, and RCPath has said a centre can be cancelled if fewer than 10 candidates are allocated, in which case you are offered a different centre. That is a reason to keep your travel bookings refundable until your confirmation letter arrives.

### Part 2: the larger travel decision

Part 2 is a practical examination, and it is the stage where overseas candidates have historically faced the biggest cost. The College says it delivers Part 2 histopathology in a small number of overseas centres, and its international pages list Irbid in Jordan, Cairo in Egypt and Dubai in the UAE as possible locations. RCPath has also invited institutions to apply to host additional overseas Part 2 centres from Autumn 2026. We found no announcement of an India centre for Part 2. If you are planning around travel, check the current centre list and the dates published for your sitting, because centre availability can change between diets.

For the Autumn 2026 sitting the College published Part 2 histopathology dates of 13 and 14 October, with results on 20 November. Our earlier post on [overseas centres for Part 2](/blog/frcpath-part2-histopath-overseas-autumn-2026/) covers how the expansion fits into an Indian career plan.

## How and When You Pay

Candidates apply and pay through the RCPath online application system during a fixed window. For Autumn 2026 that window ran from 29 May to 3 July, with a withdrawal deadline of 17 July and entry confirmation on 31 July. For Spring 2026 the window ran from 28 November 2025 to 2 January 2026. Dates for Spring 2027 had not been published when we checked, but the spring pattern suggests an application period around the turn of the year. Watch the College's examination calendar.

On refunds, the Autumn 2026 Part 1 guidance states that a candidate can withdraw before the published deadline and receive a refund, but the application cannot be transferred to the next sitting, so you must reapply. Withdrawals after the deadline are refunded only in exceptional circumstances. Do not assume a fee is refundable once the deadline has passed.

## Funding and Reimbursement

RCPath announced in July 2026 that resident doctors and locally employed doctors in England can have the fees for their first two attempts at each College examination reimbursed, for exams taken after 1 April 2026, with the claim made through the employer. This applies to doctors employed in England. It does not apply to candidates working in India, so Indian candidates should plan to self-fund unless their institution or sponsor has its own scheme.

Some candidates ask whether a sponsor can pay. The College's application process includes sponsor and supervisor requirements, so raise this with your educational supervisor early.

## Is the Investment Worth It?

The value depends on your destination. For UK work, the published NHS consultant salary scale in England on the 2003 contract currently runs from £113,565 at the starting threshold to £150,569 after 14 years, according to the BMA's 2026 scale. FRCPath is part of the route to UK specialist training and consultant posts, but it does not by itself give you a UK job. GMC registration, a right to work and a competitive post are separate requirements.

In India, the Fellowship is a credential that sits alongside an MD or DM. Many candidates pursue it for academic standing and for international mobility, not as a replacement for the Indian qualification. If you are weighing it against NEET-SS or INI-SS, our post on the [NEET-SS and FRCPath dual plan](/blog/frcpath-autumn-2026-application-neet-ss-dual-plan/) explains how the two timelines can run together.

## Practical Ways to Reduce the Total

1. Apply in the first week of the window, so you can choose a centre and book travel before prices rise.
2. Keep Part 1 and Part 2 travel bookings flexible until confirmation letters arrive.
3. Share accommodation with other candidates sitting the same diet, since Part 2 candidates are usually in the same city on the same days.
4. Buy only the textbooks and Blue Books you will use repeatedly, and use institutional library access for the rest.
5. Choose one structured question bank early, instead of buying several. A subscription such as the [FRCPath Part 1 histopathology course](https://elearningfrcpath.com/frcpath-part-1-histopathology-course) or the [pathology MCQ bank](https://elearningfrcpath.com/pathology-mcq-2026) costs far less than a failed sitting and a second fee.

## Exam Pearls and a Budgeting Mnemonic

**Mnemonic: BEST**

- **B**ook early inside the application window.
- **E**xchange rate: convert on the day you pay and add a margin.
- **S**tudy resources: shortlist before you buy.
- **T**ravel: keep it flexible until the centre is confirmed.

**Pearls**

- The fee is per sitting, so preparation quality is the cheapest way to control cost.
- Fees change every year. Do not copy last year's number into this year's budget.
- RCPath allows four attempts at each part before permission from Council is needed for another, so a repeated sitting adds a full fee.
- Part 1 content is unchanged by the move to British Council test centres. Practise on a screen, not on paper.

For a wider view of how the two parts fit together, read the [FRCPath Part 1 and Part 2 strategy guide](/blog/frcpath-exam-strategy-guide-part1-part2/) and the [official exam guide](https://elearningfrcpath.com/frcpath-exam-guide-2026) on our course site.

## Practice MCQs

These are sample questions in the style of Part 1. They test the molecular and diagnostic reasoning that the exam rewards, not the fee rules above.

**Q1.** A 52-year-old woman has a right-sided colonic adenocarcinoma. Immunohistochemistry shows loss of MLH1 and PMS2 with retained MSH2 and MSH6. BRAF V600E testing is positive. Which is the most likely explanation?

- A) Germline MLH1 mutation (Lynch syndrome)
- B) Sporadic MLH1 promoter hypermethylation
- C) Germline MSH2 deletion
- D) POLE exonuclease domain mutation

<details>
<summary>View Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: B — Sporadic MLH1 promoter hypermethylation**

Loss of MLH1 and PMS2 together with a BRAF V600E mutation points to somatic MLH1 promoter methylation in a sporadic tumour. BRAF V600E is not expected in Lynch syndrome, so a positive result argues against germline MLH1 mutation (A). Loss of MSH2 and MSH6 would be needed for C. POLE mutation (D) gives an ultramutated tumour with proficient mismatch repair staining.

</details>

**Q2.** A diffuse astrocytic tumour in a 38-year-old shows an IDH1 R132H mutation and homozygous deletion of CDKN2A/B. Under the WHO 2021 classification of CNS tumours, what is the correct designation?

- A) Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, CNS WHO grade 2
- B) Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, CNS WHO grade 4
- C) Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype, CNS WHO grade 4
- D) Oligodendroglioma, IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted

<details>
<summary>View Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: B — Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, CNS WHO grade 4**

In the 2021 classification, an IDH-mutant diffuse astrocytoma with homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion is graded 4 regardless of necrosis or microvascular proliferation. Option C is wrong because the tumour is IDH-mutant, and the term glioblastoma is now reserved for IDH-wildtype tumours. Option D requires 1p/19q codeletion, which is not stated.

</details>

**Q3.** A small round blue cell tumour of the femur in a 14-year-old shows diffuse membranous CD99 staining. Which translocation product is most characteristic?

- A) SS18::SSX1
- B) EWSR1::FLI1
- C) PAX3::FOXO1
- D) FUS::DDIT3

<details>
<summary>View Answer & Explanation</summary>

**Answer: B — EWSR1::FLI1**

Ewing sarcoma is defined by an EWSR1 fusion, most often with FLI1, and shows strong membranous CD99. SS18::SSX is synovial sarcoma, PAX3::FOXO1 is alveolar rhabdomyosarcoma, and FUS::DDIT3 is myxoid liposarcoma.

</details>

## FAQs

**How much are FRCPath exam fees in 2026?**

For histopathology, the 2026 RCPath fees are £749 for Part 1 and £1,576 for Part 2, giving £2,325 in exam fees if you pass both at the first attempt. Fees are revised every year, and the April 2024 Part 1 fee was £707, so check the current schedule on rcpath.org before you apply. These figures cover the examinations only and do not include travel, study materials or time away from work.

**Is the FRCPath fee different for international candidates?**

The 2026 fee document we found lists one fee per part for histopathology and does not show a separate overseas rate. The extra cost for international candidates comes from travel to the test centre or practical exam venue, not from a higher fee. Older College fee schedules did show different overseas rates for some exams, so confirm on the current schedule before you pay.

**Where can Indian candidates sit FRCPath Part 1 and Part 2?**

For Autumn 2026, Part 1 was offered at British Council test centres including New Delhi. Part 2 histopathology is held in the UK and in a small number of overseas centres, listed on the College's international pages as Irbid, Cairo and Dubai, with the College seeking more centres from Autumn 2026. No India centre for Part 2 had been announced when we checked, so verify the current list.

**Can I get a refund if I withdraw from the exam?**

For Autumn 2026, RCPath said candidates could withdraw before the published deadline of 17 July and receive a refund, but the application could not be moved to the next sitting. After the deadline, refunds apply only in exceptional circumstances. Because deadlines differ each sitting, read the guidance for the diet you are entering and email the Examinations team promptly if your plans change.

**Does FRCPath reimburse exam fees for doctors in India?**

No. The reimbursement announced in July 2026 covers resident doctors and locally employed doctors in England, for their first two attempts, claimed through their employer. Candidates in India should plan to self-fund, or ask whether their institution or sponsor has a scheme. Keep every receipt in case a funding body asks for it.

- Fees rise yearly, so add a margin if you will sit after 2026.
- Part 1 is now a computer-based exam in British Council test centres, with New Delhi among the Autumn 2026 centres.
- Part 2 is held in the UK and in a few overseas centres, and the College is expanding the overseas list. No India centre had been announced when we checked.

- Withdraw before the published deadline if you need a refund, and confirm all figures on rcpath.org before paying.
