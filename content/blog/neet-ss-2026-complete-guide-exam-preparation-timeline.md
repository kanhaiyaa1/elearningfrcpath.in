## Common Mistakes to Avoid in the Final Stretch

I've mentored hundreds of NEET-SS aspirants, and these errors appear repeatedly:

**Mistake #1: Starting new books in November**

Stick to what you've been reading. Adding Rosai or Fletcher in the final month creates confusion without adding points.

**Mistake #2: Neglecting negative marking math**

At -1 for wrong answers, random guessing hurts you. For questions where you can eliminate 2 options, the expected value favors attempting. For complete guesses among 4 options, skip.

**Mistake #3: Ignoring image-based practice**

30-40% of questions involve images. If you're only reading text, you're not preparing for the actual exam. Practice with digital pathology resources and histopathology image banks.

**Mistake #4: Skipping mock tests**

"I'll do mocks when I finish the syllabus" — this never happens. Start mocks by Week 9 regardless of syllabus completion. They expose gaps more efficiently than re-reading.

**Mistake #5: Underestimating laboratory medicine**

QC, QA, accreditation standards, equipment principles — these appear every year. Don't skip them as "boring" topics.

## Exam Day Strategy

**December 26-27, 2026** — here's how to approach it:

### The Night Before

- Sleep by 10 PM — cognitive performance drops 25% with sleep deprivation
- No new studying — light revision of mnemonics only
- Prepare documents, admit card, ID proof
- Set multiple alarms

### During the Exam

1. **First 10 minutes:** Skim through all sections, identify comfortable questions
2. **Next 150 minutes:** Attempt known questions first, mark uncertain ones
3. **Final 50 minutes:** Return to marked questions, make educated guesses where 2+ options eliminated
4. **Last 5 minutes:** Verify answer sheet marking, don't change answers unless certain

**Time allocation per section:**
- 50 questions in ~50 minutes = 1 minute per question average
- Some questions need 30 seconds, others need 2 minutes
- Don't get stuck — mark and move

## Practice MCQs

**Q1.** A 45-year-old woman undergoes core biopsy for a breast mass. IHC shows ER positive (90%), PR positive (60%), HER2 score 1+, Ki-67 15%. What is the molecular subtype?

A) Luminal A-like  
B) Luminal B-like (HER2 negative)  
C) HER2-enriched  
D) Triple negative  

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

**Answer: A) Luminal A-like**

With ER strongly positive, HER2 negative (score 1+), and Ki-67 <20%, this fits Luminal A-like criteria. Luminal B-like would have Ki-67 ≥20% or PR <20%. The distinction matters for adjuvant chemotherapy decisions — Luminal A-like tumors often spare patients from chemotherapy based on gene expression assays.

</details>

**Q2.** In NEET SS 2026 exam pattern, a candidate attempts 180 questions, getting 140 correct. What is the final score?

A) 560  
B) 520  
C) 480  
D) 540  

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

**Answer: B) 520**

Calculation: Correct answers = 140 × 4 = 560. Incorrect answers = 40 × (-1) = -40. Final score = 560 - 40 = 520. This illustrates why accuracy matters more than attempting every question.

</details>

**Q3.** Which molecular alteration defines the diagnosis of AML regardless of blast percentage according to WHO 5th Edition?

A) FLT3-ITD mutation  
B) NPM1 mutation  
C) CEBPA mutation  
D) RUNX1 mutation  

<details>
<summary>View Answer</summary>

**Answer: B) NPM1 mutation**

WHO 5th Edition eliminated the 20% blast threshold for AML with NPM1 mutation. Studies showed NPM1-mutated myeloid neoplasms with <20% blasts have outcomes similar to AML and benefit from AML-type therapy. This is a frequently tested concept in NEET-SS.

</details>
